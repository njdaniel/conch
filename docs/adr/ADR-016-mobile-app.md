## Context

The first real tester of the private alpha was on a phone (ADR-013's context). The browser serves phones today with a one-panel layout, Chat / Channels / Voice navigation and a compact voice strip ([mobile-browser-layout.md](../design/mobile-browser-layout.md)), and it is honest about its limit: hold-to-talk works only while the page is active, and nothing is promised about a locked or backgrounded phone. The desktop track (ADR-007; [linux-desktop.md](../design/linux-desktop.md), [windows-desktop.md](../design/windows-desktop.md)) is Electron with a native helper and has no phone form.

On 2026-11-01 Nick asked to expand the mobile planning and begin work, in parallel with the browser alpha and without displacing it (#757). #757 fixes the product: open a member invite, confirm the server, sign in, browse the channels one belongs to, send text and images, join voice explicitly, keep the voice session through app switching and screen lock, return, leave, sign out; mute, deafen, listen-only, open microphone, foreground on-screen push-to-talk, optional privacy-preserving push, authorised screen-share viewing; both OSes, with Android as the first physical lane and iOS evidence gathered independently.

What the server offers a phone today is inventoried in the design's §1 at main `7472b65`: every route of that story exists except making a member link (community slice I6a), the channel directory (I3b, I4), the per-person voice switch (I5) and any push registration (M3, M11). The voice rules a phone must obey are the server's existing ones: one participant identity `p<id>` per person, a 15 s join token, transmit reports, entitlement re-read on every pass, companion identities issued only to a present `p<id>` on the same machine. conchd does not refuse a second voice session for a person; LiveKit displaces the earlier connection, and the browser and the helper already stop on that displacement rather than fight it.

A phone app adds a toolchain the charter does not name, dependencies the allowlists do not carry, and OS behaviour (foreground services, audio sessions, interruptions, power management) that no page read for this proposal lets anyone promise from a desk. That is why this ADR proposes a track with experiments, not a stack.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **A native phone app exists as a track**, for Android and iOS, delivered through the child issues of #757, with the browser kept as the no-install client on both. It is a client of the public REST and WebSocket API and gets no route, field or server rule of its own (ADR-001, D6): anything the phone needs is added to the API first and is usable by every client. Its wire types come from `pkg/schema` through generation and fixture conformance, never by hand (CLAUDE.md rule 2); a React Native client imports `web/src/schema/` as it stands, a Kotlin or Swift client needs a new emitter under the protocol designer before any screen is written.
2. **The experiment candidate for M4 and M5 is React Native with the LiveKit React Native SDK**, bare (no Expo Go), at the pins in the design's §7.2 (React Native 0.88.1, `@livekit/react-native` 3.1.0, the repository's `livekit-client` 2.22.3 and Node 24.21.0, JDK 17, Android SDK Platform 35, Xcode 27.2). **The named fallback is a Kotlin client and a Swift client** on the LiveKit Android 2.29.2 and Swift 2.19.0 SDKs, chosen at M6 if either experiment shows the shared layer cannot own the audio lifecycle at the moments the OS demands. The PWA and a WebView wrapper are compared in the design's §2 and are not the candidate. **M6 records the production stack from the two receipts; this ADR does not.**
3. **One voice participant per person per room, moved by the person.** The person's `p<id>` connection in a room is the one authoritative microphone and playback owner there, on whichever device holds it: that is the scope of today's enforcement (`internal/server/voice.go` line 31, "one principal, one connection per room"; LiveKit's displacement acts within a room). A phone that joins a room while another device holds `p<id>` **in that room displaces that device by the person's explicit act**: the phone reads presence, sees its own principal already in the room, says so before Join (and before Watch), and the displaced device stops and does not rejoin, as the browser and the helper already do on `duplicate_identity`. No server rule is added and no simultaneous voice devices are offered in one room. **Across rooms the rule does not reach:** a desktop in channel A's voice and a phone joining channel B's voice both stand and both may transmit, and no client can see that cheaply (occupancy carries counts only). A **global owner** (one room per person at a time, enforced by conchd when it issues a session, with a way for clients to learn where the person is in voice) is a server design, a wire addition and a behaviour change for every client; it is a future issue and a question for Nick (M1-4), not part of this decision. The companion and viewer identities `p<id>.screen` and `p<id>.view` exist for one machine's screen media beside its native voice and **are never a phone's way into a room**. Whether a person's own second device may ever listen without displacing is likewise a later design and a later question for Nick.
4. **Identity on the phone is a credential.** One credential per phone install, made by a redeemed invite, a password sign-in or a one-use sign-in link made on another device (all existing routes), kept in the platform keystore and sent as a bearer header, never in a URL except the single-use WebSocket ticket the server issues for that purpose. It is listed and ended with the existing "my sign-ins" routes from any device. Revocation, channel removal and loss of the voice entitlement each end the phone's media and sockets on the server's existing timers; the phone treats them as sign-out or as leaving and never retries a dead credential. Server profiles follow the desktop's model: a random local id bound to a canonical origin and a verified principal.
5. **Transmit behaviour.** Foreground on-screen push-to-talk only, on the browser's gate **plus two additions the phone must build** (the browser's `lost()` shuts push-to-talk only, and its gate opens on the first loud sample once unblocked): a lifecycle block that shuts the gate in both modes on background, lock and interruption, and a resume latch that needs quiet and then a fresh onset before open mode may open again. A press opens, a release closes after the tail; touch cancel, loss of focus, app switch, screen lock and an audio interruption shut the gate with no tail; nothing opens it on resume; the microphone track is muted before it is published and again after publishing and after any republish (the browser's order, #501), so joining without a press sends nothing; a press past the bound shuts it, and the bound on a phone is **30 s**, the desktop figure Nick set on 2026-10-28. The open microphone is level-triggered and never always-on; in the background it is **off by default** and is offered on an OS only after M4 or M5 has shown capture continues there with a persistent system indicator, interruptions shut it, and the cost is measured. Staying connected in the background with the gate shut is the goal and is pending per OS; where it fails, the phone leaves voice on backgrounding and says so. **Background, lock-screen, notification, headset and hardware push-to-talk are unsupported** until an experiment shows press and release both arrive and the bound holds on that OS. Opening the app, a channel, an invite or a notification never joins voice.
6. **Push is optional** and is M3's design (#1108): no push is a supported configuration; a notification never joins, unmutes or opens a session; a VoIP push is not a chat notification; approval notifications stay on ntfy and approval decisions stay out of the phone app in this phase (D7).
7. **OS floors for the experiments:** Android 12 (API 31) with Android 14 as the primary lane; iOS 16.4 with the current iOS on Nick's iPhone as the primary lane. A combination is **supported** only when an M4 or M5 receipt and then an alpha acceptance record (M14, M15) say so for that OS **and device model**; vendor power management is measured per model.
8. **Isolation of the experiments.** M4 and M5 live under `spikes/mobile/android/` and `spikes/mobile/ios/` (a **new top-level `spikes/` directory**, as #757 names it; the existing spike under `docs/design/spikes/` is an inventory script, these are buildable projects; Nick may direct them under `docs/design/spikes/mobile/` instead), each with its own lockfile, a spike-local dependency allowlist and licence report that Nick signs in those PRs, outside `make check`, `web/package.json`, `go.mod` and `voice/`. No production `mobile/` directory, no shared-module import by path and no CI leg before M6. No credential, address, device identifier or audio in any receipt.
9. **No guests, no store.** The phone offers no guest entry point (the server has none, #1005). Distribution for the experiments is a hand-installed APK and a free-provisioning or TestFlight development build; any store listing, tester invitation or release is its own owner authorisation (#757).
10. **Out of scope in this phase** (the same list as the design's §8 and M1-9): phone screen broadcasting; the camera; global, background or hardware push-to-talk; DMs; administration beyond what every member already has; SSO; guests; store submission; approval decisions.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Why not, or why not yet |
| --- | --- |
| **Keep the PWA as the phone client and stop** | It is kept, and it is not enough for the story: the page cannot own the audio session or a foreground service, it shuts the gate on `visibilitychange` by design, and no page read gives footing to promise voice through a screen lock from a browser. If the report's M-L1 row unexpectedly passes on both OSes, Nick hears it before M6 and the track's priority is reconsidered. |
| **A WebView wrapper (Capacitor) around the React client** | The native shell without the native media stack: capture and playback stay in the WebView under its permission and background rules, which are the least documented of the four options. It would reuse every view, and the views are what M2 wants rewritten for a phone anyway. |
| **Separate Kotlin and Swift clients from the start** | The most controllable and the most expensive: two codebases, two schema emitters, two sets of gates, before any evidence that the shared layer falls short. Named as the fallback, with the same experiment matrix, so that M6 can choose it on evidence. |
| **Refuse a second voice session on the server (409) instead of displacement** | Turns "picked up the phone after leaving the desktop in voice" into a dead end that needs the other device to leave first. The desktop designs refuse a second **companion** because a share can be stolen; a second **voice** connection by the same person is the same person. Displacement is already safe because every client stops on it. |
| **Let a phone use the viewer identity to listen beside the desktop** | The viewer was designed as the same machine's second window; from a second device it is the simultaneous-device behaviour this ADR does not offer, reached through a side door. If wanted, it is a server design and Nick's question. |
| **Expo managed workflow for the experiments** | Expo Go cannot load `react-native-webrtc`; development builds can and may be M6's tooling choice. Not needed to measure the OS, which is what M4 and M5 are for. |
| **Decide the production stack now** | #757 puts it at M6 after both device receipts, because the deciding facts (background audio, interruptions, routing, power management) are measured, not read. |

## Consequences

- **Nothing is built by this ADR.** It authorises M4 and M5 to add the §7.2 dependencies under `spikes/mobile/` once Nick signs each line, and it fixes the policies (one owner, hand-off, foreground PTT, background open mic off, floors) that M4, M5 and M2 design against.
- **A toolchain is added to the repository at M6**, with its own `make` leg, allowlists and licence gate in the shape of `web/`'s, and CI time grows. `make check` must not skip an existing leg to make room.
- **Two desk findings become browser and desktop follow-ups:** the hand-off warning before Join and Watch belongs on the browser and the desktop too (parity of words with Decision 3); and if the credential-making routes let no client name its credential, a device label is an additive request field under `schema-change` with a security review. The design's §10 lists them; none blocks M4 or M5.
- **Push brings a third party into an optional path** (FCM, APNs, or a Web Push service); the deployment invariant is untouched because no push is a supported configuration. M3 says the rest.
- **Security review** precedes the merges of sign-in and profiles (M7), push (M11, M12) and production voice lifecycle (M9, M10); M4 and M5 carry a review of their credential handling.
- **The alpha's isolation premise is unchanged:** the phone is one more client under the same visibility rules; `e2e/isolation` gains rows when a phone client exists, not before.
- **The roadmap, ADR-007's table and lifecycle paragraph, and ADR-006's platform clause** need the edits named under Amends; accepted ADRs are not edited, so they are recorded here, and the roadmap line is Nick's.
