# Conch alpha: a guide for testers

Thank you for testing Conch. Conch is a place to chat, talk and share a screen with
your group, in your web browser. Nothing needs installing.

This guide is for the alpha's pre-release builds. Each build's version is shown under
**Settings** (the gear at the top right of the Conch page; for example
`Version v0.1.0-alpha.1`). Please quote it whenever you tell us about a problem.

A few parts of this guide are still being finished. They are marked like this:
*(lands with #444)*. The marks come out once those parts are in a build; if your
build does not have one yet, skip that part.

## What you need

- A computer with Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, or a phone or tablet with its usual
  browser, kept up to date. All of them should work: finding out whether they do is
  part of the test.
- Headphones, if you will use voice. They stop the others hearing themselves back.
- The invite link you were sent.

Please keep invite links to yourself. Anyone who has an open invite link can use it
to join.

## 1. Open your invite

1. Open the link you were sent, in the browser you want to use Conch in.
2. The page says **You are invited to Conch**, and shows:
   - who invited you;
   - which channels you will be in;
   - whether voice is included;
   - when the invite expires.
3. The long code at the end of the link disappears from the address bar as soon as
   the page opens. That is on purpose: the invite is not kept in your browser's
   history.

If the page says **This invite no longer works**, the invite has been used, has
expired or has been closed. Ask the person who sent it for a new one. A personal
invite works only once, so do not pass yours on.

If the page says you are already signed in as someone else, choose **Continue as a
new person** only if you mean to make a second account. Otherwise choose **Go back**.

## 2. Choose a name

1. Type the name you want the others to see, under **Your name**.
2. Choose **Join**. You arrive in Conch, in your first channel.

Names are unique on the server. If the page says **That name is taken. Choose
another.**, someone already has it, or a name that looks too much like it (for
example the same name in capitals, or with a zero for an "o"). What you typed stays
in the box, so you can change it and try again. Names that begin with `hidden-` are
reserved.

You can change your name later: **Change your name**, under **Settings** (the gear at
the top right of the page). You can change it up to five times a day. Your old messages then show your new name.

## 3. Stay signed in

- You stay signed in on this browser, even after closing the tab or the whole
  browser. Every tab of Conch in this browser is signed in as you.
- Your sign-in lasts as long as you keep using it. If you do not use Conch for a long
  time (90 days, unless you were told otherwise), it ends.
- A private or incognito window forgets you when it closes. So does a browser set to
  clear site data on exit. Use a normal window.

Under **Settings** (the gear at the top right of the page) there are two buttons:

- **Sign out** ends your sign-in on this browser, in every tab.
- **Sign out everywhere** ends every sign-in you have, on every device.

**Please do not sign out unless you mean to leave.** There is no password. Once you
sign out, you cannot sign back in by yourself: ask the person running the test to
let you back in.

To use Conch on a second device or another browser, ask the person running the test.
They can give you a sign-in for your account, to paste on the sign-in page. A new
invite would make a second, separate account instead.

If the page says **Signed out: the credential is no longer valid**, your sign-in has
ended: it expired, or your access was removed. Tell the person running the test.

## 4. Chat

- Your channels are listed on the left (in a row at the top on a phone). A number after a
  channel's name is how many messages you have not read yet.
- Type in the box at the bottom of the conversation and press Enter to send.
- The bar at the top of the page shows your name, the server and the connection (on a
  phone, your name and the connection). The connection says **Connected** when all is
  well. **Members**, beside it, shows who is in the channel.
- If your connection drops, it says **Reconnecting**, and the conversation says **This
  conversation may be out of date**. You do not need to do anything: when the
  connection comes back, Conch catches up on the messages you missed, each one once.
- If a message of yours says **Not confirmed: check the conversation before
  retrying**, look whether it arrived before sending it again.

## 5. Voice and the microphone

Voice is in the **Voice** strip along the bottom of the page. It says whether you are in
voice, whether your microphone is in use, and who else is in voice.

1. Open the channel you want to talk in, and choose **Join voice**.
2. Your browser asks whether Conch may use your microphone. Choose **Allow**. If you
   blocked it by mistake, allow the microphone in the browser's settings for this
   site, then join again.
3. You are in. Everyone in the channel's voice is listed. Whoever is talking is marked
   **(speaking)**.

There are two ways to talk. Choose one under **Voice options**, **Microphone**:

- **Push to talk** (the first choice). Hold the talk key while you speak, and let go
  when you have finished. The talk key is the backtick key, `` ` ``, which on most
  keyboards is just below Esc. **Change talk key**, under **Voice options**, sets another. On a phone or a touch
  screen, press and hold **Hold to talk** instead.
  - The talk key works only while the Conch tab is the one in front, and not while you
    are typing in a box. Switching to another tab or app counts as letting go.
- **Open microphone.** Your microphone opens by itself when you speak. Use the slider
  under **Voice options** with the level meter in the strip: move it until the meter
  opens the microphone for your voice but not for the noise around you.

The other buttons:

- **Mute** stops you being heard. **Unmute** undoes it.
- **Deafen** stops you hearing anyone, and mutes you too. **Undeafen** undoes it.
- **Leave voice** leaves.

Good to know:

- On a phone, voice keeps working only while the Conch page is open on the screen.
  Locking the phone or switching apps cuts it.
- You can be in voice on one device at a time. Joining from a second device takes
  over, and the first one says another device took over.
- **Voice is unavailable right now; chat still works** means the voice service is
  down for a moment. Conch keeps trying by itself.

## 6. Share your screen *(lands with #444)*

You can share your screen with the others in a channel's voice. One person shares at
a time.

1. Join voice (section 5).
2. Under **Voice options**, **Share your screen**, choose **Text and pictures**
   (documents, code, slides) or **Video or a game** (moving pictures).
3. Read the note under the buttons: it says what sound your browser can share (see the
   table below).
4. Choose **Share screen**. Your browser asks what to share: a browser tab, a window or
   the whole screen.
5. To share sound, tick the browser's box for sharing audio (it may say "Share tab
   audio" or "Share system audio").
6. Afterwards, the page says whether sound is being shared.

To stop, choose **Stop sharing**, or use the browser's own stop button. Leaving voice
stops it too.

**Careful:** if you share the whole screen, or the browser window Conch is in, the
others see the Conch page too, chat included. Sharing one tab, or another app's
window, avoids that.

If someone else is already sharing, the page says who. Wait until they stop.

**What sound your browser shares:**

| Your browser | Share a screen | Sound it shares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chrome or Edge on Windows | yes | a tab's sound, or the whole computer's sound when you share the whole screen |
| Chrome or Edge on a Mac or Linux | yes | a tab's sound only |
| Firefox or Safari | yes | no sound |
| iPhone or iPad | no | no |
| Chrome on Android | not known yet: please try it | no |

This is what the browsers say they do. Part of the test is finding out whether it is
true, so please tell us when it is not.

## 7. Watch a share, also on a phone *(lands with #444)*

- When someone shares, the Voice strip says who is sharing, and shows **Watch**.
  Choose it to see the share, above the conversation.
- **Full screen** fills your screen. On a phone, the picture fits the screen.
- The share's sound has its own volume and **Mute share**, apart from the voices.
  Deafen silences the share too.
- On a phone, the browser may not play sound by itself. Choose **Tap to play sound**.
- **Stop watching** closes the share.

You can watch without using a microphone: choose **Watch only** instead of **Join
voice**. Your browser does not ask for the microphone. If you want to talk later,
choose **Use microphone**.

On a phone, keep the Conch page open on the screen while you watch.

The three share columns are about screen sharing *(lands with #444)*. Like the table
in section 6, this one says what the browsers say they do. Tell us what really
happens on yours.

## 9. Report a problem *(lands with #446)*

When something goes wrong, report it from inside Conch. It takes a minute, and it
sends us what we need to find the cause.

1. Choose **Report a problem**, under **Settings** (the gear at the top right of the
   page). It is also offered when the page cannot reach the server.
2. Answer the questions:
   - **What you did**;
   - **What you expected**;
   - **What happened instead**;
   - **How often does it happen?** (every time, sometimes, or once so far).
3. Choose **Review report**. The page shows **exactly what will be sent**. Besides your
   answers, that is: the version, your browser and system, your screen size, whether
   you were in voice or sharing, and a list of recent connection events (when the
   connection dropped and came back, and which requests failed). It never includes
   your messages, the channel names or your sign-in.
4. Choose **Send report**, or **Edit** to change something first.
5. The page says **Thank you. Your report was sent as number** and a number. Keep the
   number, in case we ask you about it.

Please do not put passwords, invite links or anyone's private details in your answers.
Every report is read by the person running the test before anything from it is made
public, and names are taken out.

If you cannot reach Conch at all, tell the person running the test instead. Say what
you did, what happened, the version from **Settings** if you saw it, and
your device and browser.

## 10. What is known not to work yet

- **The talk key works only while the Conch tab is in front.** A key that works while
  a game or another app is in front needs a desktop app, which comes after the alpha.
- **On a phone, voice and watching stop when the page leaves the screen** (the phone
  locks, or you switch app).
- **Not every browser can share, or share sound.** See the tables in sections 6 and 8.
- **Signing out cannot be undone by you**, and a private window forgets you. See
  section 3.
- **No files, search or threads in the browser yet.** They come after the alpha.
- **No notifications.** Conch does not alert you to new messages while its tab is in
  the background, though the channel list counts them.
- **On a slow connection, a message you send may show twice for a moment** while it is
  being sent. It settles to one.
- **A share stops if the sharer's connection drops** for more than a few seconds. The
  page says so; share again. *(lands with #444)*
- **If a share is stopped for you**, for example by the person running the test, the
  page does not yet say why. *(lands with #444)*

## 11. Switching servers

You may be invited to more than one Conch server. Each server has its own address,
such as `https://chat.example` and `https://other.example`, and the address is what
your browser goes by:

- Your browser keeps a separate sign-in for each server's address. Signing in, or out,
  on one never changes another.
- Keep one tab, or one bookmark, per server. To switch, go to that server's tab.
- An invite to another server opens that server, and makes your account there.
- One browser holds one sign-in per server. To be signed in to the same server as two
  different people, use a second browser.

**If you also use the terminal app** (`conch`): it keeps its sign-in in a settings
folder. For a second server, or a second person, give each its own folder, by setting
`CONCH_CONFIG_DIR` to that folder before you sign in and whenever you run `conch`.
The project's README explains it, under "Human side".
